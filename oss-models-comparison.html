06 Sell It — Playbook for Sales & SA

Key points to capture, by role. One pitch, one proof stack, one objection table.

🗣️ The 30-second pitch: "Every hyperscaler will serve you open-weight models — eventually. The question is when, on whose engineering, and under whose governance. Databricks is the only platform serving the exact latest OSS models (GLM 5.3, Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) on our own infrastructure, day-0 — not via a partner's engine — with automatic prompt caching, published latency guarantees, and spend governance built in. The model is a rental. The platform is the asset."

🎯 Sales — lead with these 3

  • Day-0 OSS, self-engineered. Azure needs Fireworks (a partner engine) for new open models; Bedrock trails a generation; Vertex two. We host on our own fleet — newest checkpoints, no partner dependency
  • Cost: on par or cheaper. K3 matches Bedrock's best tier ($3/$15), beats Azure by ~9%; DeepSeek Flash at $0.14/$0.28 is commodity-cheap. Cache-read discounts apply automatically across the whole lineup
  • Governance included, not bolted on. Traffic through Unity Gateway = per-team spend caps, audit trails, model routing. Bedrock charges separately for Guardrails; Azure governance lives in another stack

🔧 SA — prove with these 3

  • <50ms overhead per request (published), Priority queue-jump tier, Provisioned Throughput with SLA + fine-tuned model serving on the same endpoints
  • Automatic prompt caching on every open-weight model — batch, pay-per-token, provisioned; Agent Bricks/Genie inherit it. Bedrock's K3-only explicit caching costs +25% to write
  • No region-prefix gymnastics. Serverless endpoints live in the workspace; data boundary is clean. Bedrock's cheap global tier routes requests to any commercial region — an FSI compliance conversation
ObjectionResponse
"Bedrock just added Kimi K3 too"True — one model, and their first OSS with caching. Ask them for GLM 5.3 or DeepSeek 4.1: they stop at GLM 5 / V3.2. One model arriving late ≠ a day-0 platform.
"We're an Azure shop — Foundry is native"Fair, and Fireworks on Foundry is good — it's a partner engine, several older FW models are already PTU-only (pay-per-token deprecated), excluded from EU Data Boundary, no FedRAMP/PCI. If data gravity is in your lakehouse, inference belongs next to it.
"Fireworks direct is cheaper than everyone"Check the K3 numbers: FW on Azure is $3.30/$16.50; we're $3.00/$15.00 with automatic caching. Direct Fireworks = another vendor, another governance gap, no UC integration.
"GPU speed records are on Cerebras/Groq"Specialized silicon, different comparison set — no enterprise lakehouse, no governance. We publish <50ms overhead + SLA tiers and will benchmark your workload on request (official notebook exists).
"Open models are risky for FSI"On our platform they run inside your governance boundary: UC permissions, inference tables, spend caps, PII guardrails via Gateway. Azure's Fireworks route is explicitly outside EU Data Boundary and FedRAMP.
"We'll just wait for our cloud to catch up"Model generations are moving quarterly. Every quarter of waiting = paying frontier prices for tier-3 tasks. The 4-lever cost story (up to 90%) starts with model choice — which requires the models.

❓ Discovery questions (qualify fast)

  • Which open models are you evaluating — and does your current platform have those exact versions today?

  • Who audits your model calls today — and is it runtime enforcement or after-the-fact logs?
