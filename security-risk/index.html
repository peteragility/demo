How to read it

CVSS shows published severity out of 10, such as 9.3/10 or 10.0/10. Red scores are critical (9.0–10.0); amber scores are high (7.0–8.9). — means a score is unpublished or not applicable, including some CA incidents. The CVSS version and vector are in the row details. Scoring reference ↗

Act now counts open risks with known exploitation and code execution, authentication bypass, credential theft, or ransomware use. The Risk priority sort puts these first, followed by other exploited risks, critical scores and reviewed urgent notices. Choose CVSS highest to sort by numerical severity. Confirm exposure and business impact in your environment.

KEV means CISA confirms exploitation. Absence from KEV does not establish safety. EPSS estimates exploitation probability over the next 30 days, with its own score date. These signals do not prove that your systems are affected.

Source checked ≠ fix independently verified. Branch-specific fixes are shown only where explicitly documented. Confirm distribution backports with your vendor. A materially changed advisory reopens an earlier assessment. “Source changed” requires a fresh reading of the vendor notice.

A focused watchlist, not an asset scanner or a complete CVE inventory. It tracks recent KEV additions, important older exploited flaws, and current vendor security releases. No system is marked affected automatically.
