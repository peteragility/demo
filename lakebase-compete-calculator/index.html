How to read it

▲ competitor costs more than Lakebase · ▼ cheaper · = within 1%. Tap a row for its sizing, rates and notes. Columns split each bill into compute, the HA standby, read scale, storage, backups/PITR and I/O.

Every vendor is sized to the same peak RAM. Same memory isn't the same speed: CU, ACU and vCPU differ, and Databricks says CU performance "will depend on the data and query complexity". Benchmark before a final call.

Model rules
  • Lakebase compute:
    • Scale-to-zero off: the min is billed for 730 h at the Always-On minimum-usage rate.
    • Scale-to-zero on: everything is billed at the autoscaling rate, for active hours only.
    • Above the min: usage (band × load × active h) is billed at the autoscaling rate.
    • HA and replicas: each HA secondary bills the primary's CU-hours; replicas bill the primary's CU-hours × replica load.
    • Discounts: the promo and your discount apply to compute only.
  • Lakebase storage: DB GB × the database rate, plus PITR history (changed GB/day × window × the PITR rate). Snapshots aren't modeled.
  • Provisioned vendors: the smallest class with RAM ≥ peak, running 730 h (active hours if stop-when-idle is on; AlloyDB always 730 h). Serverless v2 gets Lakebase's capacity profile (1 ACU ≈ 2 GiB).
  • HA conventions:
    • Aurora: +1 replica.
    • RDS: Multi-AZ rates.
    • Azure: ×2 compute and storage.
    • Cloud SQL: HA rates.
    • AlloyDB: 2 nodes.
  • Backups: AWS and Azure bill changes × window beyond 100% of DB size. Cloud SQL bills DB + changes. AlloyDB bills DB + changes beyond 7 days.
  • Aurora: the row uses whichever of Standard and I/O-Optimized is cheaper and names the other.
  • Azure SQL Database (General Purpose): the cheaper of provisioned (smallest vCores with 5.1 GB/vCore ≥ peak RAM, 730 h) and serverless. Serverless is billed on memory/3 GB over Lakebase's capacity profile. HA = zone redundancy; replicas = extra databases.
  • Commitments (instance compute only):
    • AWS RI: −33% / −53%.
    • Azure reserved: −40% / −60% (PG Flexible), −21% / −33% (Azure SQL provisioned).
    • Google CUD: −25% / −52%.
    • Not covered: burstable classes and Serverless v2.
  • Excluded: taxes, support, data transfer, private connectivity, proxies. Snowflake Postgres is sized but not priced: its credit rates are only in Snowflake's Service Consumption Table PDF.
Billing gotchas
  • Lakebase:
    • Default billing: new production branches have scale-to-zero on (24 h timeout), so a busy DB stays on the autoscaling rate.
    • Always-On rate: turn scale-to-zero off, and after 24 h the min bills at the minimum-usage rate (25% lower). Raising the min restarts the 24 h clock.
    • Dev branches: shorten the timeout, or overnight idle won't suspend.
    • HA: no scale-to-zero, and secondaries run at ≥ primary size.
    • Not discounted: storage and PITR bill at list.
    • Extra charges: synced tables bill pipeline DBUs; transfer and private connectivity are billed separately.
  • Aurora: Standard bills every I/O request. I/O-Optimized costs ~30% more per instance and 2.25× the storage rate. Storage HA isn't compute HA: fast failover needs a billed replica.
  • Serverless v2: higher unit price than provisioned. A tier 0–1 reader scales with the writer. No RIs.
  • RDS: Multi-AZ doubles instance, storage and IOPS rates. Each replica brings its own storage. gp3 includes 3,000 IOPS below 400 GB. Stopped instances restart after 7 days.
  • Azure: the standby duplicates vCores and storage. Burstable has no HA. P-tier storage grows only in 2× steps and can't shrink.
  • Azure SQL Database:
    • Engine: SQL Server, so moving to Postgres means a schema and T-SQL migration.
    • License: list prices include the SQL license; Hybrid Benefit removes it on provisioned only.
    • Stop and pause: provisioned databases can't be stopped; serverless auto-pauses.
    • Zone redundancy: an uplift on compute and storage.
    • Read scale: on General Purpose, read replicas are full databases.
  • Cloud SQL: HA doubles CPU, RAM and storage rates. Backups have no free allowance.
  • AlloyDB: an HA primary is 2 nodes. Logs kept beyond 7 days bill as backup.
  • Snowflake Postgres: credits/hr per size. Replica replication is billed as data transfer. Burstable sizes have no HA and a 100 GB cap.
