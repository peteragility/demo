How to read it

Rows are sorted from lowest to highest monthly cost. ▲ costs more than Lakebase · ▼ costs less · = within 1%. Tap a row for its sizing, rates and notes. Columns split each bill into compute, the HA standby, read scale, storage, backups/PITR and I/O.

Every vendor is sized to the same peak RAM. Same memory isn't the same speed: CU, ACU and vCPU differ, and Databricks says CU performance "will depend on the data and query complexity". Benchmark before a final call.

Model rules
  • Scenarios (7 common cloud OLTP uses; each sets pattern, size, data, I/O, HA and replicas):
    • Web / SaaS: daily peaks · 8 CU · 200 GB · moderate · no HA.
    • E-commerce: daily peaks · 16 CU · 500 GB · heavy · HA + 1 replica.
    • Internal app: business hours · 4 CU · 100 GB · moderate · no HA.
    • AI agent app: spiky · 8 CU · 50 GB · light · no HA.
    • Lakehouse serving: steady · 16 CU · 300 GB · light · 2 replicas, no HA (data can be re-synced from the lakehouse) · hourly incremental sync.
    • Core OLTP: steady · 32 CU · 1 TB · heavy · HA + 1 replica.
    • Dev / test: intermittent · 2 CU · 20 GB · light · no HA.
  • Load pattern: Lakebase minimum / average load while busy, as a share of peak:
    • Daily peaks, 24×7: 25% / 50% (peak ≈ 2× average).
    • Steady: 50% / 70%.
    • Business hours (12 h × 5 days): 25% / 60%.
    • Spiky: 12.5% / 30%.
    • Intermittent (6 h × 5 days, scale-to-zero): 6% / 30%.
    • Constant at peak: 100%, 24×7 (like-for-like with provisioned databases).
    • Range rule: Lakebase's max − min ≤ 16 CU; above 64 CU it's fixed-size.
  • Lakehouse sync (Lakebase synced tables):
    • Billing: pipeline-hours × pipeline DBU/hour (Advanced, default 2, an assumption) × the published serverless pipeline $/DBU. Your Databricks discount applies; the Lakebase promo doesn't.
    • Daily snapshot: full copy at ~2,000 rows/s per CU.
    • Hourly incremental: changes at ~150 rows/s per CU, at least 5 min per run (both rates from the Lakebase docs, ~1 KB rows assumed).
    • Continuous: 730 h/month, billed even when nothing changes.
    • Competitors: they need the same data movement (a custom reverse-ETL job, or a tool such as AWS Glue at $0.44/DPU-hour), priced at the same compute. The difference is effort and governance, not compute.
  • Why these defaults:
    • Use cases: application development is the leading Postgres use case (State of PostgreSQL 2024).
    • Steady vs variable: AWS positions provisioned databases for steady workloads and serverless for variable ones, such as promotions, multi-tenant SaaS, new apps and dev/test (Aurora docs).
    • Lakebase scenarios: agent memory, feature serving and serving lakehouse data follow the Lakebase docs.
    • Caveat: these are assumptions, not your workload's measurements.
  • Lakebase compute:
    • Minimum: billed 730 h at the Always-On minimum-usage rate. With scale-to-zero (Dev / test), active hours are billed at the autoscaling rate instead.
    • Above the minimum: usage while busy is billed at the autoscaling rate.
    • HA standby: bills the same CU-hours as the primary.
    • Read replicas: each bills 50% of the primary's CU-hours.
    • Discounts: the promo and your discount apply to compute only.
  • Lakebase storage: database GB plus PITR history (changed GB/day × window).
  • Other vendors:
    • Sizing: the smallest memory-optimized class with RAM ≥ peak, billed 24×7. Burstable classes are used only for Dev / test at ≤ 8 GB.
    • Serverless options: Aurora Serverless v2 and Azure SQL serverless follow Lakebase's capacity profile (1 ACU ≈ 2 GiB).
  • I/O intensity (per CU of peak, plus share of data changed per day):
    • Light: 25 IOPS, 1% changed.
    • Moderate: 100 IOPS, 3% changed.
    • Heavy: 400 IOPS, 10% changed.
    • Peak IOPS: 3× the average (at least 3,000).
    • What it drives: Aurora Standard I/O charges, RDS gp3 / Azure SSD v2 extra IOPS, and backup / PITR size.
  • HA conventions:
    • Aurora: +1 replica.
    • RDS: Multi-AZ rates.
    • Azure PG: ×2 compute and storage.
    • Azure SQL: zone redundancy.
    • Cloud SQL: HA rates.
    • ClickHouse: +1 standby.
  • Backups: AWS, Azure PG and Azure SQL bill changes × window beyond 100% of DB size. Cloud SQL bills DB + changes. ClickHouse (Beta) doesn't bill backups.
  • Aurora / Azure SQL: the row uses the cheaper of Standard vs I/O-Optimized (Aurora) or provisioned vs serverless (Azure SQL), and names the other.
  • Commitments (instance compute only):
    • AWS RI: −33% / −53%.
    • Azure reserved: −40% / −60% (PG), −21% / −33% (SQL).
    • Google CUD: −25% / −52%.
    • Not covered: burstable classes, Serverless v2 and ClickHouse.
  • ClickHouse Postgres: the smallest memory-optimized size whose RAM ≥ peak and local NVMe ≥ DB size, at Scale-tier list price, with the 50% Beta discount when ticked.
  • Excluded: taxes, support, data transfer, private connectivity, proxies, and lakehouse ETL / sync costs. ClickHouse Beta pricing may change at GA.
Billing gotchas
  • Lakebase:
    • Default billing: new production branches have scale-to-zero on (24 h timeout), so a busy DB stays on the autoscaling rate.
    • Always-On rate: turn scale-to-zero off, and after 24 h the min bills at the minimum-usage rate (25% lower). Raising the min restarts the 24 h clock.
    • Dev branches: shorten the timeout, or overnight idle won't suspend.
    • HA: no scale-to-zero, and secondaries run at ≥ primary size.
    • Not discounted: storage and PITR bill at list.
    • Extra charges: synced tables bill pipeline DBUs; transfer and private connectivity are billed separately.
  • Aurora: Standard bills every I/O request. I/O-Optimized costs ~30% more per instance and 2.25× the storage rate. Storage HA isn't compute HA: fast failover needs a billed replica.
  • Serverless v2: higher unit price than provisioned. A tier 0–1 reader scales with the writer. No RIs.
  • RDS: Multi-AZ doubles instance, storage and IOPS rates. Each replica brings its own storage. gp3 includes 3,000 IOPS below 400 GB. Stopped instances restart after 7 days.
  • Azure: the standby duplicates vCores and storage. Burstable has no HA. P-tier storage grows only in 2× steps and can't shrink.
  • Azure SQL Database:
    • Engine: SQL Server, so moving to Postgres means a schema and T-SQL migration.
    • License: list prices include the SQL license; Hybrid Benefit removes it on provisioned only.
    • Stop and pause: provisioned databases can't be stopped; serverless auto-pauses.
    • Zone redundancy: an uplift on compute and storage.
    • Read scale: on General Purpose, read replicas are full databases.
  • Cloud SQL: HA doubles CPU, RAM and storage rates. Backups have no free allowance.
  • ClickHouse Postgres:
    • Beta pricing: 50% off today; egress and longer backup retention may be charged at GA.
    • Storage: it's local NVMe, so a bigger database forces a bigger instance. Each standby is a full instance.
    • Resizes: old and new instances run in parallel and are both billed.
    • Region: not offered in Hong Kong.
